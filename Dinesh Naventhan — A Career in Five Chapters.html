Prologue · 2004–2006

It started on a mainframe

Macrosoft India · Software Engineer

My first job was converting COBOL applications and redesigning data models, moving mainframe systems onto distributed platforms. Unglamorous work, but it taught me the lesson every later chapter leaned on: you can’t modernize what you don’t deeply understand.

Chapter one · 2006–2013

Where a swipe becomes a decision

Cognizant for JPMorgan Chase · Lead Business Analyst, Core Payments & Authorization

I moved into the heart of the card: authorization. I helped lead the insourcing of TS2 card processing from TSYS, giving Chase tighter control of authorization, settlement and post-transaction processing.

Along the way I built real-time POS authorization, delivered mobile payments and real-time clearing, and coordinated Visa and Mastercard releases.

Approval accuracy, latency and fraud risk, all balanced in the milliseconds of a single tap.
Chapter two · 2013–2023

Helping customers when life gets hard

Cognizant for Capital One · Senior Product Manager, Customer Resiliency & Recovery

A decade on the other side of the card: customers who are struggling. I designed an ML-powered early financial hardship detection platform so outreach could start before delinquency, and led the move of FICO Debt Manager to an in-house, cloud-native recovery platform.

I also ran large portfolio migrations, including Costco Canada and GM Small Business, with zero customer disruption.

~32%operating cost reduction on the recovery platform
~200%portfolio growth in two years
4.01→4.53customer satisfaction score
Chapter three · 2023–present

Making partnerships feel like one product

JPMorgan Chase · VP, Senior Product Manager, Co-Brand Credit Card

Back at Chase, I took on co-brand: Amazon, United, Disney and Avios. The job is to make a partner’s brand and Chase’s platform feel like a single, seamless product.

I led the shift from batch to real-time enrollment and lifecycle orchestration, and designed AI-assisted, event-driven notifications.

~40%faster partner onboarding via API-first platforms
22%higher engagement, with less alert fatigue
Chapter four · now

The Apple Card parity build

JPMorgan Chase · Apple Card program

The hardest promise to keep is the invisible one: customers should notice nothing. For Apple Card, my team is building parity, so everything cardholders rely on today at Goldman Sachs works the same way on Chase’s platform.
